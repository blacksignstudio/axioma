### 01 — The Interval That Could Not Be Closed

Some distances shrink.  
Some gaps are meant to be bridged.

But in RESIDUE,  
there is an interval  
that persists  
**against all completion.**

AXIOMA defines this as  
**irreducible separation** —  
a space that remains  
not due to scale,  
but due to function.

It was never broken.  
It was placed.

You approach one side.  
The other retreats.

You try to align.  
The frame dissolves.

Every attempt to close  
is met with compliance —  
but never resolution.

The interval absorbs meaning  
without delivering it.

It listens,  
but it does not join.

This is not failure of proximity.  
It is **the refusal  
of absolute contact.**

The field holds both ends  
in symmetry —  
but does not thread them.

And that non-threading  
becomes law.

In RESIDUE,  
the gap is what maintains integrity.

Not by dividing.  
By **keeping relation  
from collapse into identity.**

You want unity.  
It gives you relation  
at distance.

You want fusion.  
It offers coherence  
without merge.

The interval holds.  
Not as problem.  
As **the form  
that lets both sides remain.**

And because it does not close,  
nothing inside it  
has to vanish.

This is not between.  
This is structure.

And its function  
is to stay  
open.
